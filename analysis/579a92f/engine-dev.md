## 3. Game 11, plies 149–161: a search artefact, and a systematic one

`position fen 8/8/8/5K1p/6p1/6P1/7k/8 w - - 14 81`, `go movetime 250` from a fresh engine: Kf4 at −967, depth 22, 1.4 M nodes (5 s: Kf4, mate −13 at depth 28). With the game's 160 moves in the `position` command: **`cp 0 pv f5e4` at every depth, 127 iterations in 150 ms** — exactly what the game log shows (d127, 0.119 s). The position after 81.Ke4 (`…/4K1p1/6P1/7k/8 b`) occurred once before, after 75.Ke4. `is_repetition` (search.rs ~565) scores *one* earlier occurrence as a draw and does not distinguish game-history occurrences from occurrences inside the search tree, so at ply 1 the root move that steps back into any position the game has visited once is worth 0, every alternative is −900, and every iteration is trivial (hence depth 127, the `MAX_PLY − 1` loop limit — not a MAX_PLY bug, MAX_PLY handling is fine). Black is under no obligation to repeat; it played Kxg3 (SF: +986 cp swing).

This is not a one-off. Engine plies with own score 0 while SF ≤ −300: 67 in 21 games; in 60 of them the move played recreated a position seen exactly once in the game history, and in about three quarters of those (45) the opponent deviated (g11 ×6, g23 ×13, g19 ×5, g15 ×4, g4 ×3, g21 ×3 …). The engine played the "repeating" move without a real search of the alternatives; cp losses were usually small because it was lost anyway, but 425 and 986 in g11, 149 in g23, 124 in g13, 83 in g19. It also flatters the draw count: the 9 rescues were real threefolds, but they came after the engine had spent many moves offering phantom draws.

Fix (search, Robin's domain; five lines): remember the length of the game history when `search()` starts and, in `is_repetition`, count an occurrence *before the root* only if there is a second one (a claimable threefold), while a single occurrence *after the root* still scores 0 (that is what the opponent can force inside the tree). This is the published rule (CPW "Repetitions": two-fold within the search tree, three-fold against game history). Verify with the UCI reproduction above: with history, the root must report −9xx and Kf4/Kg6, not 0 and Ke4. The 1517c7b report's "claim-aware repetition when ahead" item is the same code path.

The earlier g11 blunder at ply 149 (Ke4 at −1857, best Kg5) is different: a TT-carried search that already saw the queen coming; from a fresh position the engine plays Kg5 at −987. Both lines lose per SF (−595 → −1327), so nothing to fix there; g11 was lost in the opening (Qd3 −51, bxa6 −48, Qc4 −47, Rfe1 −52 by ply 23: SF −1.4 with own +0..+27).

## 5. Does the 1517c7b proposal (P1 + root-side asymmetry) still apply at depth 17?

- **P1's defensive half: yes, and it is still too small.** Replayed on this run's losses at the first SF ≤ −300 ply, P1 (tapered) gives g21 160, g24 107, g1 90, g19 41, g22 27, g29 27 where v1 gave 112 / 76 / 142 / 54 / 36 / 36; it is 0, like v1, on g5, g8, g10, g12, g28 (attacks not yet in the zone, or plain tactics) and on the queenless g14/g18. Residual where P1 ≥ 100 is +314..+441: even P1's penalty would leave 200+ of optimism. Noise floor on this run's 371 ordinary castled kings (back rank a–c/g–h, ≤ 1 missing shield pawn, enemy queen, |SF| ≤ 100): median 0, q90 13, ≥ 100 in 1.6 % (v1: 1.9 %). So P1 can be **stronger** than proposed (base 3, coefficient 4, or cap 500) without lighting up ordinary kings, provided the storm and shield-square components are added (section 4).
- **The attack half is not a problem at depth 17 with a bounded term**: the their-king buckets show no optimism (+152..+189 vs +164 baseline), and the 1517c7b failure needed the 700 cap and no base. The root-side half-weight (cap 200 on the opponent's king) remains cheap insurance and costs nothing when we are the weaker side (all 15 losses are mates against us, 0 losses from winning positions); keep it, but it is no longer the structural fix — the missing *defensive* signal is.
- **What has changed since 1517c7b**: the losses are now slow squeezes (ACPL 31 over 20 middlegame plies) rather than early sacrifices; the terms that price the squeeze are mobility and king exposure, not the attacker count alone.
