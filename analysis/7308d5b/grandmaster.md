# Grandmaster review of runs `7308d5b` and `7308d5b-s1`

Reviewer's brief: our engine (material + piece-square tables, alpha-beta with a transposition table, depth 7-8 in 0.25 s) against Stockfish 19 at UCI_Elo 1700/1800/1900. Run A (`7308d5b`, classical openings) 14/4/12, run B (`7308d5b-s1`, Sicilian/French/Caro/Scandinavian) 20/3/7. Only wins count. This review concentrates on the 15 games in which Stockfish (full strength, depth 14) rated the engine at +1.5 or better and the engine did not win: losses A2, A3, A6, A8, A19, A29, B6, B15, B20, B22 and draws A11, A25, B5, B8, B24. It builds on `analysis/498eace/grandmaster.md`; that review's items 1 (king safety), 2 (trapped pieces) and 5 (trade policy) are evidently still not in the engine and are still the biggest leaks.

## Summary

The engine does not lose won positions in the endgame; it loses them in the middlegame, and it loses them with its own king. In 8 of the 15 games (A2, A8, A19, A29, B6, B20, B22, B24) the materially-ahead engine was mated or hunted because its queen and pieces went pawn-hunting on the far wing (19...Qxb3 in A8, 12...cxb2 in B6, 13...Qxb2/17...Qc4 in B22, 29...Rxb2/34...Qc3 in B24, 17.Qxa7 in B15, 28...Nxa2 in B20) or because its king was treated as an endgame piece while the opponent still had rook plus two minors (Ke3 on move 7 and Kh4-h5-h6-h7 in A29; Kd5 and Kf4 in B20). Twice it castled long straight into an open file (16.O-O-O in A19, 10...O-O-O in B6). The engine's own score at the moment it threw the game was +180 to +460 in every one of these; Stockfish's was −6 to −10.

The second leak is the search horizon on forcing sequences: a five-ply back-rank mate (29...Rxd2?? in A6, own eval +440), a perpetual check from a +10 position (21.Qxd2?? in A25, own eval +1550), a knight fork after a check (9...Bg4+?? in B8), a two-move mating net (18...c5?? in B22). The third is refusing to simplify when ahead (27...Qa5? instead of Qxc2 in A6, 36.Qb6? instead of Qxd7 in A3, 21.Qxd2 instead of 21.Qe4 in A25). Real endgame-technique failures (passed pawn never pushed, king not activated, rook not behind the passer) account for only two games: A3 and A11.

An important negative finding about the draws: contempt would not have saved a single one of the five. A25 was a forced perpetual. In B5, B8 and B24 the engine was already lost (−6.9, −8, −10 by Stockfish's count) when Stockfish-1700/1900 chose to repeat; those draws were gifts. A11 ended by insufficient material after the a-pawn was lost. The wins slipped ten to thirty moves before the repetition in every case.

Reading guide: evals in brackets are Stockfish depth 14 from White's side; "own" is the engine's score from its own side at the move it played, taken from the summaries.

## Game notes, grouped by mechanism

### 1. The king is left alone while the pieces go pawn-hunting (A8, B22, B24, B6, B15)

**A8 (Black vs 1700, Italian).** After 13...Nb4 Black was a clean pawn up with the better structure (−2.01). 14...Rfc8? (Rfe8) 15...Rc2? (Nd5) 16...Rxa2 17...Rxa1 18.Rxa1 was acceptable, but 19...Qxb3?? (−243, best Qb5) put the queen on b3 with the knight on c6, bishop on e6 and rook on d8: not one black piece could reach g7 or h6. White had Qf4, Ng5, Bd2 all pointing at the king. Stockfish-1700 then blundered with 20.Ne4?? (−4.69 for us) and the engine had 20...Qd5! with a won game; it played 20...Rd8? and after 21.Qg5 the decisive 21...h6?? (−403, best Kh8, position now 0.00) weakened the very square the attack needed. 22.Nf6+ Kh8? 23.Qh4 gxf6?? (−762, own +460) 24.Bxh6 and the king was walked to e1 and mated. Strategic: the queen went to b3 for a pawn while three enemy pieces stood next to the king. Tactical: gxf6 at depth 6.

**B22 (Black vs 1800, Caro-Kann).** After 12.exf6 Be3+ 13.Kh1 the black king on g8 was about to lose its g-pawn (fxg7 Kxg7). Best was 13...g6 (−129 lost by 13...Qxb2?). After 14.fxg7 Kxg7 the king had only f7 and h7 in front of it and White had Qh5/h4, Nh3 heading for f4-h5, and Rf1 on the f-file. The engine's queen made five moves away from the king: Qb6-b2-d2-d3-c4. 17...Qc4?? (−475, best Qg6: the queen goes home) and 18...c5?? (−1056, own +230, best f6) allowed 19.Nh5+ Kg8 20.Qf6 and mate on g7/h7. Strategic (queen distance from own king when the enemy has Q+N on that side); the final tactic was only two moves deep.

**B24 (Black vs 1900, Caro-Kann).** +2.09 after 24.Ra4?. From there: 24...b5?? (g6), 26...Nc7?? (Kh7), 27...Ne6?? (Qb3), 28...Qc7?? (−594, own +120, best Qb4), 33...Qb4?, 34...Qc3?, 36...a5?, 37...Qe3+?, 38...Nf6?? (−781, own +285, best Qe4+). The whole sequence is one idea: White had Ne5, a bishop coming to c4/h3xe6 and a rook on d6/d7, all aimed at f7 and the g8 king with h6 played; Black's queen and rook went to b2, b4, c3 and e3 collecting queenside pawns. Stockfish repeatedly wanted ...h5 and ...Kh7 (get the king off the a2-g8 diagonal). Stockfish-1900 had mate after 39.Bxf7+ and took the perpetual: a gift.

**B6 (Black vs 1900, Sicilian).** Up +2.86 at move 9 after Stockfish's 7.Na2?. Black's pawns were a7, c6, c5 (doubled after Bxc6+ bxc6) — no b-pawn, so the b-file was half open for White's rook and the a4-e8 diagonal for the queen. 10...O-O-O?? (−256, best Bd6 and keep the king on e8) castled into exactly that, and 12...cxb2?? (−794, own +215, best Qd7) opened it further for a pawn: 13.Qa6+ Kb8 14.Qb5+ Kc7 15.Qa5+ Kc6 16.Bxb2 and the king was hunted from c6 to h4 over forty moves. The engine's own score never dropped below +2 while it was being mated. Pure evaluation: castling side chosen by the king PST (+30 for c8) with no reference to which files are open and where the enemy queen is.

**B15 (White vs 1800, French).** After 13...d4? White was +3.77 with Qh6 against a king on e8 and a d4 pawn to collect with 14.Rad1. Instead 14.Qh7?? (−393, own presumably high for "attacks Rg8") sent the queen to the corner; 17.Qxa7? (−95) grabbed the a-pawn; 19.Na4?? (−406, best Ne4) put a knight on the rim to defend it, and after 19...Nd7 the queen on a7 had no squares (Bb7 blocks, Nd7 covers b6/b8). 20.Bxd4?, 21.Nxb6? and 23.Nd5+? were increasingly desperate attempts to free it; 26.Qxa8 Bxa8 gave it up for rook and bishop and the rest was a 40-move lost defence. This is the same "queen alone in the enemy camp" pattern as game 27 of `498eace`.

### 2. The king is played as an endgame piece while the opponent still has rook plus two minors (A29, B20, plus A2 and A19 with queens on)

The engine then played 7.Ke3, 12.Kd4, 18.Kf4, 21.Kxf5, 22.Kg4, 23.Kh4 — in a position where Black had two rooks, two bishops and a knight. Stockfish-1900 kept missing the kills (23...Re4+? instead of Nd2 let White reach +2.18) but the king on h4 was a permanent target: 28.Be8? (gxf5 needed), 31.Bb3? (Rxh2 wins a bishop), 33.gxf5? 0.00, 37.axb5?? (−282, own +?) took a pawn while ...Bxf6 removed the f6 pawn that shielded the king, 39.Kh5?? (Kh6), 42.h5?? The engine's endgame king PST rewards every step toward the centre and the enemy pawns; it has no notion that R+B+N is mating material.

### 3. Forcing lines beyond the horizon (A6, A25, B8, B5)

**A6 (Black vs 1900, start position).** Two pawns up and +1.9 at move 26. 27.Qc2 Qa5? (−92, best Qxc2: with queens off Black's two extra pawns and bishop pair win by hand; the engine's own eval prefers to keep the queen). 28.Rf4?? (Stockfish blunder) Bxf2 and Black was +5.6. 29.Qa2 — the queen and the rook on f4 now form a battery on f7/f8 — and 29...Rxd2?? (−1583, own +440, best Rf8) allowed 30.Qxf7+ Kh8 31.Qf8+ Rxf8 32.Rxf8#. A five-ply mate at depth 6 with a search that reports +440: the checking moves must be getting reduced or the mate must be scored inside quiescence wrongly. Luft (...h6 at any point from move 20) would also have made it a non-issue.

**A25 (White vs 1700, Petroff).** Stockfish-1700 blundered a piece on move 1 and another on move 9 (9.Bg2?? Rfe8?? — the engine hung its e4 knight for a move and was not punished). At move 20 White was +10 with an extra rook, bishop and knight. 20...Rd2 21.Qxd2?? (−1011, own +1550, best Qe4) 21...Qg4+ 22.Kh1 Qf3+ 23.Kg1 Qg4+ — perpetual. The check sequence to threefold is 8-10 plies; depth 6 never sees the repetition, and the static evaluation has no idea that a king with an open g-file and no defenders on the second rank is being perpetually checked. 21.Qe4 covers g4 and f3 and offers the queen trade a rook and two pieces up.

**B8 (Black vs 1700, Sveshnikov).** Stockfish-1700 played 3.Bg5?? and the engine won a piece (−4.87 for White). At move 9 (9.Kd1, knight on c2 attacked) the simple 9...Nb4 keeps everything. Instead 9...Bg4+?? (−540): 10.Kxc2 Qxa8 11.Nc7+ Ke7 12.Nxa8 and the piece was gone, because after the check the queen capture on a8 runs into a fork. Stockfish still rates the position level (+0.08) because the a8 knight is trapped; the engine then never went to get it (12...Bh5? instead of d5, 14...Kd8?, 16...Kc8?, 19...Kb8? — ...f5 and ...Re8 were best five times) and the h8 rook never moved until move 31. From +5.7 to −8; Stockfish then repeated instead of playing 39.Re7.

**B5 (White vs 1900, Sicilian).** +2.39 after 22...Qe2. 23.Rfe1? (−140, best b4, kicking the c5 knight) took the rook off f2's defence; 24.e5? (Qe3), 25.Qh3? (Rbc1), 26.e6? (Qa3+) pushed the passed e-pawn three times with no support while the queen sat on h6/h3 and Black's Qc2 and Nd3 were aimed at f2 and e1; 27.exd7?? (−721, own +180, best Rf1) and 27...Qxf2+ 28.Kh1 Nxe1. Best moves at every step were about the queen returning (Qe3, Qa3+) or defending f2/the back rank (Rbc1, Rf1). Stockfish-1900 then failed to convert −9.9 and repeated. Note the tactical part (Qxf2+ and Nxe1) is only three plies, so this is not primarily a depth problem: the engine's search preferred a line that wins the d7 rook and did not weigh the resulting king exposure.

### 4. Real endgame technique (A3, A11)

**A3 (White vs 1800, Sicilian).** After 13.Bxc5 bxc5 14.Qxa6 White was a pawn up with a passed a-pawn and Black's king still on e8 (+2.0, peak +2.67 at move 21). Stockfish's plan was consistent: a4 (move 21), a3 (26), b3 (32), b4 (46) — push the passer. The engine's moves: 17.Qb6? 18.h4? (−102, a pawn thrown forward in front of its own king for nothing; it fell to 39...Qxh4+), 19.Nb5 20.Nc7+ 21.Nb5? 22.Nc3? 25.Ne2 26.Ng3? (a six-move knight tour), 23.Rab1? (−162, best Nxe5 removing the centralised knight). 36.Qb6? (−81) refused 36.Qxd7+ into an ending still better for White; 37.Re2?, 39.Qe3?? (−171) lost h4, and with the queens on and his king stripped (50.f3?) White lost. This is the one loss where "passed pawn not pushed" was the main reason.

**A11 (White vs 1900, Italian).** The +3.34 middlegame was thrown by 12.c3? (−88, a3 wins a piece) 15.Qxd7? (−138, best Ng3: the queen took a bishop and let ...Qxe2 in) 16.h3? (−130, Rae1). The rook ending (4 v 3 with an outside a-pawn) was objectively level, but Stockfish-1900 handed White +3.95 (51...Kg5??) and +3.4 (64...Ra2+?). Each time the engine ran the wrong way: 52.h4+? (−305), 53.Rc6? (−154), 54.Kf2? (−307), 55.Kf3? (−291) — best was Kd4 four times running, the king walking to the passer; 48.a6? (−193, Rd8 first), 64.a7? (−274, Ra7 first: rook behind or beside the pawn before pushing), 66.Rc7?? (−367, Kd3) and the a-pawn was lost. Draw by insufficient material at move 110. The endgame-king PST alone cannot tell the king "go to the passed pawn"; it needs a distance term.

In eleven of the fifteen the losing tactic was the consequence of a position the evaluation had already misjudged: the engine only needed to see 23...gxf6 (A8), 18...c5 (B22), 38...Nf6 (B24), 12...cxb2 (B6), 27.exd7 (B5) or 42.h5 (A29) because it had already parked its queen on the wrong wing or its king in the open. Compared with `498eace`: the opening problems (queen sorties, Bxc6+ reflexes, uncastled king in the opening) are much reduced with the new openings; the losses have moved later, into positions the engine had already won. King safety and material greed remain the number-one and number-two causes exactly as in that review. New in these runs: the king-walk in queenless middlegames (A29, B20), the perpetual-check horizon (A25), and rook-ending technique (A11). Draw contempt (`498eace` item 3) is still irrelevant: no draw in these runs was taken from a better position.

## Prioritised suggestions (engine-implementable)

Ranked by how many of the 15 thrown/slipped games each would plausibly have turned into wins.

1. **King safety, both colours: pawn shield, attackers, open files next to the king, castling side.** Score: (a) −penalty per missing or advanced pawn on the three files around the king; (b) −penalty per enemy piece attacking a square in the king zone (3×3 plus two squares toward the centre), weighted by piece type and multiplied by ~2 if the enemy queen is on the board; (c) −penalty for an open or half-open file adjacent to the king when an enemy rook or queen can reach it; (d) evaluate O-O and O-O-O with the same terms so castling long with no b-pawn scores negative. Fixes B6 (10...O-O-O with no b-pawn, 12...cxb2), A19 (16.O-O-O onto the b-file, Ka1 mating net), A8 (21...h6?? with Q+N+B in the zone), B22 (Kg7 with no g-pawn, Nh5+), B24 (f7 under N+B+R), A2 (Kd7/Kc8/Kb8 with the queen on), and would have kept B5's queen at home (Qe3/Qa3+ instead of e5-e6). **6-7 games.**

2. **Check extension and mate/perpetual awareness in the search.** Extend by one ply when the move played gives check (cap the total extension per line), do not apply late-move reductions to checks or to the reply to a check, and let the repetition detection see the check sequence so that a perpetual is scored 0 inside the search. Fixes A25 (21.Qxd2?? own +1550: Qg4+/Qf3+ repetition is 8-10 plies), A6 (29...Rxd2?? own +440: Qxf7+ Kh8 Qf8+ Rxf8 Rxf8# is five plies and should never be missed at depth 6), B8 (9...Bg4+?? Kxc2 Qxa8 Nc7+), B22 (18...c5?? Nh5+ Kg8 Qf6), B24 (38...Nf6?? Bxf7+), and would have shown A19's 30.Rf4?? and A8's 23...gxf6??. **4 games outright, 3 partial.**

3. **Phase by the opponent's non-pawn material, not by "queens off".** Use the endgame king PST (centralise, approach pawns) only when the opponent's non-pawn material is ≤ one rook (or ≤ ~600 cp); otherwise keep the middlegame king PST even without queens. Add a small "king distance to nearest enemy heavy piece" penalty when the opponent has ≥ R+minor. Fixes A29 (7.Ke3, 12.Kd4, 23.Kh4, 39.Kh5, 42.h5 against R+B+N) and B20 (27...Kd5, 38...Kf4 against 2R+N+B); leaves A11 (pure rook ending) correctly in "activate the king" mode. **2 games, both losses.**

4. **Simplification bonus when ahead.** When the material balance (after the terms above) is ≥ +150 cp, add a bonus proportional to `(advantage × pieces removed from the board)` for exchanging queens and, to a lesser degree, rooks; mirror it as a penalty when behind. Fixes A6 (27...Qxc2 instead of Qa5, and no back-rank mate later), A3 (36.Qxd7+ instead of Qb6), A25 (21.Qe4 offering the trade instead of Qxd2), and would have stopped B20's 49...Rc7?? (behind, do not trade the last rook). **3 games.**

5. **Passed pawns done properly: rank bonus, "path is safe" condition, rook behind, king distance.** Bonus rising with rank (roughly 10, 20, 40, 70, 120, 200 cp from rank 3 to 7) but only the full amount when the square in front is not attacked by the enemy or is defended more than attacked; a bonus for own rook behind the passer; in endings (item 3's definition) a term for own king distance to the pawn's front square and enemy king distance. Fixes A3 (a4/a3/b3/b4 wanted for 25 moves, never played), A11 (Kd4 ×4, Rd8/Ra7 before a6/a7, 66.Rc7??), and B5 in the negative direction (e5-e6-exd7 while f2 and e1 hung would no longer look like progress). **3 games.**

6. **Trapped pieces and queen mobility, with the pawn-grab test.** Count safe squares for each minor and the queen; −large when ≤ 1 safe square, doubled in the enemy half; and, cheaply, treat "capture a pawn on rank 7/8 with the queen" as needing ≥ 2 safe retreat squares afterwards. Fixes B15 (14.Qh7, 17.Qxa7, queen lost for R+B), and the pawn-grab side of A8 (19...Qxb3), B22 (13...Qxb2, 17...Qc4), B24 (29...Rxb2, 34...Qc3), B20 (28...Nxa2); in B8 the same term, applied to the enemy knight on a8, would have told the engine to go and take it. **2 games outright, 4 partial (overlapping with item 1).**

7. **Development and rook activity while ahead.** Penalty per undeveloped minor and per rook that has not moved after move 10 while own material advantage ≥ +100 (so "cash in the advantage by bringing everything") and a bonus for a rook on an open/half-open file.
