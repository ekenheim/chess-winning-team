# Grandmaster review: `55375f9-full` (5 s/move, Stockfish 1900-2100) and `c9cb33a` (0.25 s/move, Stockfish 2100-2300)

Reviewer's brief: same eval in both runs (tapered material + PST, `king_danger` = 18 cp per missing shield pawn plus a zone-attacker table that is zero below two attackers, both zero without an enemy queen), same search (TT, null move, LMR, PVS, killers, check extension). `55375f9-full`: 27/0/3 at 5 s, depth ~13. `c9cb33a`: 21/3/6 at 0.25 s with the new time manager (soft limit 60 %, aborted iteration's best root move kept; 81 % of the clock used, depth ~10.8). Neither run's eval has a passed-pawn, rook-file or mobility term; the working tree now carries an uncommitted `pawn_rook_terms` (passers by rank, rook open/half-open file, seventh rank) that neither run played with. Only wins count. This review covers the twelve non-wins: `55375f9-full` 4, 9, 22 (losses, never ≥ +1.5); `c9cb33a` 6, 13, 17, 18 (losses), 22, 24 (losses once ≥ +1.5), 5, 25 (draws once ≥ +1.5), 15 (draw, rescue from −9.9). Evals are Stockfish 19 depth 14 from **our** side unless a PGN `[%eval]` is quoted; "(−N)" is Stockfish's cp loss for our move; "d8/0.15 s" is the depth and time the engine spent; move numbers are the annotated PGNs' own (fixed openings start from a FEN). The prior reviews' findings (passive retreats, no passer/rook/king-activity terms, wrong-bishop endings, f6 wedge, single-attacker cliff) are taken as read and not re-argued.

## Summary

1. **At 2100-2300 the games are decided by attacks on our king with the queens on, in nine of the twelve non-wins, and the attack arrives in two shapes the king-safety term cannot see: our own capture or pawn push that opens a file next to our king (c9 13 `hxg4`+`Ne1`, 15 `g4/g5/Bxg7/Bxg5`, 17 `f3/gxf3`, 5 `hxg4`), and an enemy pawn storm (55 4 `f4-f5`+`Rf3-h3`, 55 9 `f5-f4-g5-g4-h5`, 55 22 `f5-f6`+`h5`, c9 18 `f4`+`Ngf5`).** The 5 s losses are the storm shape at depth 12-14, so depth does not cure it; the 0.25 s losses are the file shape at depth 8-10, where a 4-9 ply tactic finishes it.
2. **The engine's reply to being attacked is a queen raid on the other wing, and at 2200-2300 the raid ends with the queen trapped or traded for a rook** (c9 6 `10...Qxc3` → `13.Rb1`; c9 5 `23.Qxg6` → `23...Rfh7`; c9 18 `15...Qxb4`; 55 22 `12...Qxc3 14...Qa3`; 55 4 `20...Nxa4`; c9 17 `Qc4-b3-a4`; 55 9 `Qc4-b4-b3`). Queen captures are the most expensive move category per move at 0.25 s (51 cp per queen capture against 27-41 for everything else; 20 at 5 s), i.e. the pawn is real to the eval and the queen's exit squares are not.
3. **The passive pattern persists, but its cost has changed sign.** At 1900-2100 it cost slipped draws; at 2200-2300 it is how gifts are returned: Stockfish handed the engine ≥ +1.5 five times in four games (c9 5, 22, 24 ×3, 25) and the engine converted none, each time by two to five consecutive quiet moves of the passive kind (c9 22 `Rab8, Rbd8, Rd1` with `Qe2` best all three times; c9 24 `Qa2, Qb3, Qb5, Qb2` with `Rae8`, `c3`, `Qe2` best). Both are exactly the cases the uncommitted passer/rook term and a queen-mobility term are for.
4. **Openings: not the root cause, but the Ruy Lopez Exchange as White went 3/3 at 1900-2100 and 0/3 at 2100-2300**, and all six Black non-wins are losses (White's are three draws and three losses). The engine chooses `1.Bxc6` (−33) every time because it has no bishop-pair term; the resulting half-open files and Black's free `...Bg4/...f6/...g5` storm are the file shape of point 1. Start position as Black (`1...Nf6 2.d3 Nc6`, `1...Nc6 2.Nf3 Nf6?`) and the Scotch as Black (queen raids `Qxc3/Qa3`, `Nxf2?!`+`Qxb2xa2`) are the other two.

## 2. What decides games at 2100-2300

### 2a. Our own captures open files next to our king, and the term does not notice

`king_danger` prices a *missing shield pawn* at 18 and counts *pieces* touching the king zone. Neither sees a file: after `7.hxg4 hxg4` (c9 13) the h-pawn is on g4 and still counts as shield (the prior review's `dr = 1..2` finding), the black rook on h8 attacks nothing in the g1 zone through the h-file until it reaches h2, and Qf6 attacks nothing either, so attackers = 0 and the term is 0 with a rook and queen bearing down an open file at the king. The engine then plays `8.Ne1??` (own +1.71) because the only thing the eval sees is the extra bishop. The same in c9 17: `20.f3?? Bxf3 21.gxf3??` — each capture "wins" material (a bishop) and each removes the last pawn between Black's Qe7/Rf8/Bg7 and Kg1; `Ng3` and `Qc4+` were available. c9 15 is the slow version: `5.g4`, `15.g5`, `17.Bxg7`, `19.Bxg5` — four moves that each score a pawn or PST gain and together open the g-file to Black's Rg8/Qf7/Ne7-g6; SF's `f4` and `Rd8+` were the moves that kept the file shut. c9 5 `15.hxg4` (−141) opened the h-file toward its own king for a pawn it could not keep.

This is also the 5 s losses. In 55 9 the black pawns reached f4, g4, h5 against Kg1 with f3/g2/h2 all still standing: shield count 0 missing, one zone attacker (Qg5/Qh6) → term 0 for twenty moves while SF's eval went −0.5 → −7. In 55 4 White's `f5` plus `Rf3-h3` and `Qf3-h3`: at `24...b6??` the rook on f3 is the only piece that reaches the zone → 0. In 55 22 `f5-f6` and `h5` against a king that had just castled into them (`7...O-O`, −154, best `g6`) → +2.6 SF at once. Enemy pawns on the fourth to sixth rank on the king's wing are not in the term at all; depth 12-14 at 5 s does not reach the end of a ten-move storm, and the 0.25 s version at depth 8-10 does not even reach the end of the four-ply file tactic.

### 2b. The queen raid, now punished

At 1900-2100 the queen could raid and come home; at 2200-2300 Stockfish closes the door. c9 6: `10...Qxc3` (d8, **0.15 s** of 0.25 — the soft limit stopped a critical move at 60 %) `11.Bb5!` and the queen on c3 has two squares; `11...h6 12.Bd4 Qb4 13.Rb1` and it is gone for a rook. c9 5: `23.Qxg6?? Rfh7` threatens `...Rh1#` on the h-file the engine opened at move 15, so `24.Qxh7` is forced. c9 18: `15...Qxb4` with Ngf5, Bxf4 and e5 coming. 55 22: `12...Qxc3`, `14...Qa3` (−115) while `h5`, `f6` arrive; 55 4: `20...Nxa4` while `Rh3` arrives. And the slow form: c9 17 `Qd3-c4-b3-a4` and 55 9 `Qf3-e2-c4-b4-b3`, a queen five files from its king when the storm hits, so `Qc4+` (c9 17, move 21) and `fxg4`/`a5` are the moves it needed and could not find in time.

Two things are missing, both cheap. First, the queen's *own* safety: number of safe squares (not attacked by a lesser piece, or attacked by an equal one and undefended). In c9 6 after `11.Bb5` the queen on c3 has two; in c9 5 after `23...Rfh7` the queen on g6 has none that avoid `...Rh1#`. Second, the queen's distance from its own king while the enemy has a queen and two pieces on our side of the board: the prior review's proxy for this pattern improved after the term was added, but c9 17, 55 9 and 55 4 show it still loses games at this level.

### 2c. The passive pattern persists and is now how gifts are returned

Stockfish 2200-2300 still blunders, and hard: c9 22 `5.Qa4+??` (−230), c9 24 `18.Ra1??` (−408), `20.Qf4` (−158), `22.Qc1` (−182), `24.Ne4` (−255), c9 5 `17...cxd6` (−350), c9 25 `54...Rf2??` (−449). Five gifts of ≥ +1.5 in four games; none converted. In each the engine's next two to five moves were the quiet, non-committal kind:

- c9 22, +1.7 at move 20 with the a-pawn gone and a queen sitting on a7: `20...Rab8` (−51), `21...Rbd8` (−186), `23...Rd1` (−164); Stockfish's move was `...Qe2` all three times (queen into e2 hitting e3 and f1, the rook to follow). Before that, `14...Rfe8` (−56) where `Rad8` puts the *other* rook on the open file: the wrong-rook case that a rook-file term decides.
- c9 24, +3.6 at move 22: `22...Qb3` (−199) where `c3` pushes a protected passed pawn to the seventh; `20...Qa2` (−170) where `Rae8` brings the last piece; `25...Qb2??` (−519) where `Qe2` centralises with tempo. Four queen shuffles, then `26.Qh6` and `27.Nf6+`.
- c9 5, +5.5: `22.Qg4` (−122) and `23.Qxg6` (−481) where `Qe3` and `Rfe1` develop the pieces that never moved (Ra1, Nc3, Bd3 once).
- 55 4 (not a gift, but the same shape at 5 s): `17...Bd7` (−224, best `exf4`), `21...Nc5`, `22...a5`, `23...Ra8` while `Rh3` was being prepared; 55 22 `6...Bc8` (−76), `16...Nc8` (−214).

So the answer to "does it persist" is yes, at both time controls, and the runs give the term its measure: the uncommitted `pawn_rook_terms` would score `Rad8` (open d-file, +22) over `Rfe8`, `c3` (passed, rank 6 → 65 mg) over `Qb3`, and a rook on d1 (seventh-rank bonus for Black's first rank equivalent, and the file) — three of the six slip moves above; `Qe2` needs a queen-mobility term or simply the queen-safety term of 2b scoring e2 (six safe squares) above b2 (attacked by Qc1, two safe squares).

### 2d. Depth or eval

The 0.25 s losses each contain one d7-d10 move that a d13 search finds: `8.Ne1` (c9 13, mate in 5 with one quiet move), `21.gxf3` (c9 17, `Qg5+` and `Rxf3`), `25...Qb2` (c9 24, `Qh6` and `Nf6+`), `23.Qxg6` (c9 5, `Rfh7` and `Rh1#`), `10...Qxc3` (c9 6, at 0.15 s). Five of the six `c9cb33a` losses have such a move; none of the 5 s losses do. The 5 s run instead loses to the twenty-move storms of 2a. The two layers need different fixes: the storms need the eval (items 1, 2 below); the four-ply tactics need the search not to prune around the kings (item 3). On the clock: with the 60 % soft limit, 54 of 131 engine mistakes ≥ 100 cp at 0.25 s stopped before 90 % of the budget (62 of 98 at 5 s under the old 40 % manager), and three of the decisive moves in c9 22 were the 0.20-0.22 s ones.

The openings are not the cause; the engine's *choices* in three of them recreate the shapes of section 2: the Exchange Ruy hands Black the storm plan, the start-position Black lines postpone `...e5` and put the queen out early, and the Scotch as Black gives the queen a pawn on c3/b2 to fetch. A bishop-pair term (item 4) makes `1.Ba4` and `3.Nc3` (over `Na4xb6`) the engine's own preference without a book. White draws, Black loses: the six Black non-wins are losses because in all six the enemy queen was on and pointed at our king when the raid or the passivity happened; the White non-wins include three draws because with the move the engine's king usually had one more tempo.

## 4. Suggestions, in priority order

Ranked by how many of the twelve non-wins the change addresses, with the move that would have scored differently.

1. **King danger: files and storms** (`eval.rs::king_danger`). For each of the three files at and beside our king: no own pawn on the file in front of the king → +12; no pawn of either colour → +20; either case doubled if an enemy rook or queen stands on that file (x-ray through pieces is fine). Enemy pawns on the king's three files on relative ranks 4/5/6 (from the enemy's side) → +6/+14/+28 each, and count each such pawn on rank 5-6 as one zone attacker of weight 1 so the two-attacker cliff is crossed by a pawn plus one piece. Keep the "opponent has a queen" gate. Addresses c9 13 (`7.hxg4` −? then `8.Ne1`: after hxg4 the h-file scores +40 with Rh8 on it, so `hxg4` loses to `Re1`/`Nh2` lines and `8.Ne1` no longer looks like +1.7), c9 17 (`20.f3`/`21.gxf3` each open a file at +20-40 while `Ng3`/`Qc4+` keep it), c9 15 (`15.g5`, `17.Bxg7`, `19.Bxg5`), c9 5 (`15.hxg4`), 55 9 (f4/g4/h5 storm = +48 plus attackers, so `fxg4` at move 24 and `a5` counter-play at 26-27 outscore `Qb3`/`Nc4`), 55 4 (`f5` on rank 5 and the open h-file after `...h5/Rxh5` → `exf4` at 17 and `Qh7` at 18), 55 22 (`f5`/`f6` → `g6` at 7, `Bxd3+` at 16). Six to seven of twelve.

2. **Queen safety, both directions** (`eval.rs`). Count the queen's safe squares (reachable, not attacked by a pawn/minor/rook, or attacked only by the enemy queen and defended): ≤ 1 → −120 mg, 2 → −60, and +3 per safe square above 4 up to +24. Add, while the enemy has a queen and ≥ 2 pieces on our half: −8 per file of Chebyshev distance beyond 3 between our queen and our king. Addresses c9 6 (`10...Qxc3` scores the two-square queen after `11.Bb5`), c9 5 (`23.Qxg6` → the queen has no safe square after `...Rfh7`), c9 18 (`15...Qxb4`, `16...Qc5` v `Qd8`), 55 22 (`14...Qa3`), 55 4 (`20...Nxa4`-type raids are minor-piece, not covered; the `Qf6/Qh4` walk is), c9 17 (`13.Qa4`: distance 5 → −16, `Qc4+` becomes the move at 21), 55 9 (`Qb4/Qb3`), and it scores `Qe2` over `Rd1`/`Qb2` in c9 22 and 24 (six safe squares against two). Five to seven of twelve; overlaps with item 1 on 55 4/9/22.

3. **Do not prune around the kings at 0.25 s** (`search.rs::negamax`). (a) Skip null move when `king_danger` for the side to move is ≥ 38 (the two-attacker, weight-4 entry) or an enemy rook/queen sits on an open file at our king (item 1's flag); (b) no LMR, and no reduction of the second ply, for a capture or pawn move that lands in either king zone or removes the last own pawn from a king file, and for any move by the queen when it has ≤ 2 safe squares; (c) in `iterative deepening`, when the root score fell ≥ 60 cp from the previous iteration or the best move changed at the last completed depth, start one more iteration up to 90 % of the budget instead of stopping at the 60 % soft limit. Evidence: `8.Ne1` (c9 13, d10, mate in 5 unseen), `21.gxf3` (c9 17, d8), `25...Qb2` (c9 24, d9), `23.Qxg6` (c9 5, d8), `10...Qxc3` (c9 6, d8 at 0.15 s), `6...dxe4` / `21...Rbd8` / `23...Rd1` (c9 22 at 0.20-0.22 s). Five of the six `c9cb33a` losses contain one of these; the 5 s run does not, which is the measure of what an extra ply here is worth.

4. **Material imbalances: the bishop pair, and two minors over rook and pawns** (`eval.rs`). Bishop pair +30 mg / +45 eg. Raise KNIGHT/BISHOP to 330/340 or lower the rook to 480 so that B+N (currently 650) is worth more than R+2P (700) in the middlegame. Addresses c9 24 (`6...Nxf2?!`, −194: the engine sees +50, Stockfish +1.35 for White), 55 9 (`4.Nxb6`, `8.Bxc6+`: two bishops sold for nothing), and, without a book, the Exchange Ruy: `1.Bxc6` costs the pair, so `1.Ba4` (SF's choice) becomes the engine's, and c9 13/15/17's plan for Black disappears with it. Two direct non-wins plus the three Ruy Whites indirectly.

5. **Finish and measure the uncommitted `pawn_rook_terms`, with the king and the stop square** (`eval.rs`). Halve a passer's bonus when its stop square is attacked by the enemy and not by us; add in the endgame −4 per square of our king's distance to the nearest passer of either colour (c9 24 moves 36-60: the engine's king went Kf6-e5-d5-e6-f5-e5-d6 around a bishop while White's king walked in). Then keep the term only if the fast run's ACPL while ≥ +1.5 drops. Evidence this is the right term: c9 22 `14...Rad8` (open file; played `Rfe8`), c9 24 `22...c3` (protected passer to the seventh; played `Qb3`), 55 22 `5...O-O-O` (rook to the d-file), c9 22 `23...Rd1` (the rook was right, the timing wrong: `Qe2` first). Two of the four returned gifts.

Not recommended: an opening book. It would hide items 1, 2 and 4 in the three Ruy games without fixing the shape that also loses the start-position and Scotch games as Black. Not recommended either: changing the repetition score or the draw rule; c9 15's threefold and c9 5's perpetual were both earned from lost or level positions.
