# Grandmaster review: `b46124c-full` (5 s/move, no king safety) and `03fdcdc` (0.25 s/move, king safety v3)

Reviewer's brief: same search in both runs (TT, null move, LMR, PVS, killers, check extension) on the tapered material + PST eval. `b46124c-full`: 25/4/1 at 5 s against Stockfish 1700-1900, depth ~14. `03fdcdc`: 21/5/4 at 0.25 s against 1900-2100, depth ~11.5, plus the king-safety term of `engine/src/eval.rs` (18 cp per missing shield pawn; a convex zone-attacker table that is zero until two enemy pieces attack the king zone; both zero when the opponent has no queen). Only wins count. This review covers the eleven non-wins: `b46124c-full` games 15, 21 (draws once ≥ +1.5) and 24 (loss once ≥ +1.5); `03fdcdc` games 4, 5, 15 (losses), 10, 23, 26, 27, 29 (draws; 23, 26, 27, 29 once ≥ +1.5). It builds on the earlier reviews (`analysis/*/grandmaster.md`); king safety, mating nets, king walks and trapped pieces are referenced, not re-argued. Evals are Stockfish 19 depth 14 from **our** side unless a PGN `[%eval]` (White's side) is quoted directly; "(−N)" is Stockfish's cp loss for our move; "own" is the engine's root score where the summary records it; move numbers are the annotated PGNs' own (fixed openings start from a FEN). "d8/0.24 s" is the depth and time the engine spent on that move. I re-implemented `king_danger` in Python (`scratchpad/pos.py`) to read the term's value at every slip move; the proxy counts in section 2 come from `scratchpad/ks.py` over every engine move in each run.

## Summary

1. **None of the six slipped draws was a repetition taken from a better position.** In five of them (`b46124c` 15, 21; `03fdcdc` 26, 27, 29) the engine was −3.7 to −19 when the position repeated: it had thrown the win away 20-40 moves earlier, and the threefold was a rescue that Stockfish 1800-2100 allowed. The sixth (`03fdcdc` 23) ended as bare king because the engine liquidated a won B+N ending into K+B+rook-pawn with the wrong bishop, which it does not know is drawn. So the answer to the three-way question is "none of them at the repetition"; the loss of the win happened earlier, and it has one shape in all six games: **two or three consecutive quiet moves, each −100 to −250, of the passive kind (a retreat, a queen shuffle along the back rank, a rook to a closed file, a king that stays home), in positions where Stockfish's best move was in every case the active one** (Rd7, Rbd3, Rxb2, Qf3, Ng6, Bg5, Qc4, Nc8, Ke4, Rhe8, Nb6, Qg6). There is no evidence that the engine's own eval under-rated these positions; where own scores are recorded (`03fdcdc` 4: +4.82 vs 0.00; 5: +5.91 vs 0.00) they were too high, not too low.
2. **The king-safety term did change the engine's habits** (castles in 27/30 games at a median of move 4; shield-pawn pushes cost half as much as in `73fbb69`; the "queen five squares from its king while the enemy queen sits next to it" pattern cost a quarter as much; moves with two or more of our pieces on the enemy king zone rose from 16% to 26%). **It did not change what loses games.** All four `03fdcdc` losses and the lost draw of game 10 are still king exposure with the enemy queen on, in three shapes the term cannot see: a pawn wedge on f6 (game 4), a shield count that is *raised* by a pawn capture toward the centre (19.hxg4 in game 5), and a price of 18 cp for a shield pawn that a 100 cp pawn grab always outbids (6.g4 / 11.gxh5 in game 15; the queen raids in game 10). It also brought two new artefacts: a phantom-attack bonus for pieces "attacking" pawn-defended zone squares (23.Nd3 in game 27, −218) and a cliff at one attacker that gives the search no gradient.
3. **The single `b46124c` loss (game 24) is a horizon effect specific to "repetition = 0": a perpetual that lies beyond the horizon is scored as the pawns grabbed on the way to it** (21...Qxf3+, SF 0.00, instead of 21...Qxe7, +2.2). Related, and relevant to converting wins: the engine's endgame play at depth 15-16 (game 21) shows the same passive shape as its middlegame play at depth 8, so depth alone will not fix it; the eval needs passed pawns, rook activity and endgame king activity.

Per game, what the engine was missing:

**b46 21.** After 25...Rxe8 White has two rooks against rook and bishop, king on e3, pawns a6, e4, f5 against b5, c7, f7, h5. Stockfish's plan is 28.Rd7 (seventh rank, hits c7/f7) and then Rbd3; the engine put a rook on b3 in front of its own pawn and then on b1 behind the black b-pawn, let the black king walk c5-c4-c3-b3 unopposed, and let the b-pawn reach b2 while its own a6 pawn stayed on a6 for 15 moves. At move 39 it could simply take the b2 pawn (Rxb2) and did not (39.Rf1). Every one of these moves was searched to depth 13-16 in 2.4-4.8 s: the search saw far enough; the evaluation does not know that a rook on the seventh, a rook behind a passed pawn, an enemy pawn on b2 and an active king are worth anything. The 5 s run refutes the idea that the slips are just a 0.25 s depth problem.

**03f 23.** The loss of the win is two positional trades at depth 9-11 that the eval called even: 27.Bf2 gave up g2 for c5 (a pawn for a pawn, but g2 was the only pawn that could ever meet Black's h-pawn, which then ran h5-h4-h3-h2 and cost a bishop tempo at move 41), and 31.Re3 traded rooks when the engine was a piece up for pawns but the pawns were the passed ones. Then twice the best move was Ke4 (35, 37) and the engine moved a knight and captured on c6. At move 45 it had K+B+a4 against K with the bishop on the wrong colour and did not know the a8 corner is a fortress; it evaluated the position as a comfortable plus for 70 moves (every move scored equal, so it shuffled). The engine lacks: a passed-pawn term (its own a-pawn and Black's h-pawn were both invisible), endgame king activity, and a "known draw" scale-down.

**03f 26.** After 4...O-O-O the h8 rook did not move until move 39. Stockfish recommended ...Rhe8 three moves running (22, 23, 24) while the engine's queen made six moves (Qxc4, Qd3, Qf5, Qh5, Qh4, Qh3) and its knight two. This is the "piece not improved" case: the eval gives a rook on h8 and a rook on e8 the same score if both PST entries are similar, and there is no open-file or development term to break the tie, so the search spends its tempi on the queen, which the PST does reward for moving. (The final repetition, from −4.9 under rook checks, was correct.)

**03f 27 and 29.** Both are the same shape: a pawn up (27) or with a bind (29: Ne7, Bc6, Qb3, rook e1 vs a loose Ra6/Qb8), the engine plays three passive moves in a row at depth 8. In 27 they are two retreats (Nf4-e2, Be3-c1) and a rim pawn (a3); in 29 the queen goes b3-c2-b1-d3 while the best move each time was Qc4 or Nc8 (bringing the deep knight back to safety with tempo). The king-danger term is not the driver: at all six moves our own danger is 0 before and after, and the values for Black's king do not change either (checked with `pos.py`). What changed is depth: 0.25 s bought depth 8 in these queen middlegames, and at depth 8 in a quiet position with no mobility, file or passed-pawn term, retreating moves and advancing moves score the same static value and the search picks whichever avoids a tactical dip. Proxy over every engine move made while ≥ +1.5: quiet retreating piece moves cost 93 cp on average in `03fdcdc` against 24 in `b46124c-full` and 36 in `73fbb69` (50 / 60 / 42 such moves).

**b46 15** is the pre-term pattern (queen to a7, then 14.g3 and 17.Nc4 while Black's queen and knight arrived on h3/g6) and is not repeated here; note only that the term at `03fdcdc` would have scored 14.g3 as *safer* (our danger 0 before and after) and would not have prevented it.

**Verdict on the three hypotheses.** (a) Eval thinks the position is worse than it is: no; where own scores exist they are too optimistic, and at every repetition the engine was objectively lost, so the 0 score was right. (b) Cannot find a plan: yes, in every game, and the plan it cannot find is specific: rook to the seventh or behind a passer, passed pawn pushed or stopped, king to the pawns, rook off its home square. (c) Stockfish forces a perpetual it walked into: no such case in the six draws. The nearest is the loss `b46` 24, below, where the engine walked into a perpetual by choice.

## 2. The king-safety term

### What it changed

Proxies over all engine moves (`ks.py`). `73fbb69` is the same search at 0.25 s without the term against 1700-1900; the two 0.25 s runs are the fair pair, `b46124c-full` is 5 s.

| proxy | b46124c-full (5 s, no term) | 73fbb69 (0.25 s, no term) | 03fdcdc (0.25 s, term) |
|---|---|---|---|
| games castled / median castling move | 19 / move 7 | 25 / move 8 | **27 / move 4** |
| pawn moves next to own king with the enemy queen on: count / cp lost | 48 / 1826 | 68 / 3566 | 52 / **1770** |
| non-castling king moves with the enemy queen on, not in check: count / cp lost | 22 / 933 | 51 / 2443 | 38 / 2400 |
| our queen ≥5 king-steps from our king while theirs is ≤3 from it: count / cp lost | 30 / 2213 | 19 / 2670 | 19 / **604** |
| queen pawn grabs ≥4 squares from our king, enemy queen on: count / cp lost | 16 / 715 | 14 / 1350 | 20 / 950 |
| mean number of our pieces attacking the enemy king zone after our move (we have a queen) / share of moves with ≥2 | 0.85 / 19% | 0.70 / 16% | **0.96 / 26%** |
| ACPL of moves made while ≥ +1.5 | 44.1 | 46.0 | 52.4 |

Read: the term does what a small term can. The engine castles earlier, its shield-pawn pushes are cheaper (the count is the same, the damage halved, i.e. it pushes them when it is not dangerous), and it no longer parks its queen on the far wing while the enemy queen sits on its king. It also attacks more: the term is symmetric, so our attackers on the enemy king zone are rewarded, and the engine keeps pieces pointed at the king. The costs are higher ACPL when ahead (mostly the retreats of section 1, which are a depth-8 problem, not a term problem) and king moves that still cost as much as before.

### What still loses, and why the term does not see it

**Game 4 (B, 2000): the f6 wedge.** The engine was +6.0 at move 20 and played 20...Qxf6?? (−600, own +4.82, SF 0.00, d8/0.24 s) instead of 20...gxf6, missing 21.Rah1 Bh7 22.Rxh7 Qg6+ 23.Kf1 Qxh7 24.Rxh7 Kxh7, which is eight plies and reaches a level Q+N v 2R+B ending. The term nudged it the wrong way by 18 cp (gxf6 removes a shield pawn: our danger 18 → 36) but the miss is the search's. The loss came later and is the part that matters: White's f-pawn went f4-f5-f6 (moves 38-49) to sit on f6 next to the black king with a queen and knight behind it, and the engine's replies 45...Kg8 (−125, best Rbe8), 46...b6 (−151, best a5), 47...Re5 (−248, best Rh6), 49...Ree8 (−584, best Rce8, own −1.37 while SF was −2.7 going to −8.6) sent both rooks away from the king. At ply 94 and 98 the term reads our danger as 36 with **zero** zone attackers: the f6 pawn is not a piece, the queen on d5/c6 does not touch the g8 zone, the knight on g3 does not either. A pawn on the sixth rank adjacent to the king zone is the strongest attacker on the board and the term does not count it.

**Game 5 (W, 2100): the shield count rewards hxg4, and eight queen captures.** After 12.Kxf2 the engine was +4.9 and by 14.Qxa8 +5.6. The queen then made captures on a8, b7, d5, h7 (and had made a7 earlier); the c1 bishop and a1 rook never moved in 39 moves (final position `R1B2r2`); the only developed piece was Re1. 19.hxg4?? (−584, own +5.91, SF 0.00, d8/0.24 s) allowed 19...Rf8+ 20.Kg1 Qf6 21.Re2 Qf1+ 22.Kh2 Qxe2. Note what the term said: our danger before 19.hxg4 was 36 (two shield files empty around the king on f2), after it **18**, because the pawn that captured on g4 now stands on a "shield" square two ranks in front of a king on the second rank. The shield loop counts pawns at `dr = 1..2` from the king's own rank, so a king on f2 or f3 collects shield credit for pawns on the fourth rank. 28.Qxh7+ (−349, best e4) and 31.c3 (−413, best Bd2) were played with our danger at 178 and 196: the term was screaming, but no legal move changes the attacker count, so it offers the search no gradient and the search takes the pawn.

**Game 15 (W, 2000): 18 cp is not a price.** 5.h3 and 6.g4 (−89) with Black's queen on d6, 11.gxh5 (−177) winning a pawn, 17.a3, 19.Rfb1 while Black's rook came to h3. Under the term 6.g4 costs 18 (g-file empty at g2/g3) and 11.gxh5 costs nothing more while gaining 100 in material. As the `498a47b` review found for v2, and this v3 is deliberately smaller, the term prices a shield pawn at a fifth of a pawn; a search that sees one pawn will always sell it.

**Game 10 (B, 2000, draw from −9.9): queen raid unchanged.** 8...Qxa4 (−101), 10...Qb5 (−231), 11...Qxb2 while 13.Bxf6 gxf6 14.Ng4 removed the king's cover. The proxy table shows the raid pattern is now cheaper on average, but the worst case is unchanged: three queen moves on the queenside for a pawn while the enemy has Q+N+B on the king. The draw itself was Stockfish's choice (perpetual at +9.95).

**Two artefacts the term introduced.**
- *Phantom attackers.* Game 27, 23.Nd3?? (−218, best Bd2). Black's danger by the term is 146 with the bishop on h6 and queen on g3 both "attacking" g7, which is a pawn defended by the king and bishop; retreating the bishop (Bd2) drops it to 36. The eval therefore paid 110 cp to keep a bishop on a square where it attacked nothing real, and 23...Qc4 then hit d4 and a2. Any attack on a zone square that is defended by a pawn should count at half weight or not at all.
- *The cliff.* `DANGER` is zero until two pieces attack. In game 27 at moves 29-31 and game 29 at 30-32, Black had exactly one attacker on our zone and the term was 0 for every candidate move; in game 5 at moves 28-31 it was 178-196 for every candidate move. A term that is flat across all legal moves cannot steer a move choice; it only shifts the level.

### The `b46124c` loss (game 24, B, 1900): a perpetual beyond the horizon

After 17.Ra1?? the engine was +1.2 and after 19...Qf6 +2.2, with White's king on f2 and the black king safe on c8. 21.Bxe7 attacked the d8 rook; 21...Qxe7 keeps a piece for a pawn and the attack. The engine played 21...Qxf3+ (−174, d11/4.75 s, SF 0.00) 22.Kg1 Qxg4+ (−139) 23.Kf2 Qf3+ 24.Kg1 Qg3+ 25.Bg2: two pawns for the piece and a position where its only continuation is perpetual check. Within eleven plies of checks and captures the line never repeats, so it is scored as "two pawns up with checks", not as 0. Then it declined the perpetual it had earned (27...Nxe1, −159, best Nh2+), sat its rook back on d8 (29...Rd8, −239, best b5; d12 in 2.65 of 5 s), and lost. The lesson for the repetition rule: scoring a repetition as 0 is only as good as the horizon; a line of checks that grabs material and ends in a perpetual the search does not reach is systematically over-valued. The same shape produced 28.Qxh7+ in `03fdcdc` game 5.

## 3. Suggestions, in priority order

Ranked by how many of the six slipped draws each would have converted, then by the losses.

1. **Passed-pawn evaluation for both sides** (`eval.rs`). Bonus by relative rank, e.g. mg {0, 5, 10, 20, 35, 60, 100}, eg {0, 10, 20, 40, 70, 120, 200}, halved if the stop square is attacked by the enemy and not by us, plus in the endgame ±(distance of each king to the stop square) × 5 so the side whose king is nearer scores it. Enemy passers enter with the sign flipped, which is what makes 39.Rxb2 (b46 21) and stopping ...h4-h3 (03f 23) visible. Add an enemy pawn on the sixth rank adjacent to the king zone as a zone attacker with weight 2 (03f 4). Converts: b46 21, 03f 23, 03f 29 (36.b6 was the winning push), and it is the missing half of game 4's defence.

2. **Rook and minor-piece activity** (`eval.rs`). Rook on an open file +20 / half-open +10 mg, on the seventh rank with the enemy king on the eighth or enemy pawns on the seventh +30, rook behind a passed pawn (either colour) +20; a rook still on its home square after move 15 with a rook-file pawn on its own second rank −15. Knight and bishop mobility (safe squares not attacked by enemy pawns) at 3 cp per square, capped. The mobility term is what turns 29.a3 / 30.Nfe2 / 31.Bc1 (03f 27) and Qc2-b1-d3 (03f 29) into losing candidates at depth 8, and Rhe8 (03f 26) and Rd7/Rbd3 (b46 21) into winning ones. Budget: 10-15% nps; the retreat statistic (93 cp per retreat when ahead) says the engine is losing far more than a ply's worth.

3. **Known draws and endgame king activity** (`eval.rs`, the phase already exists). Before the material score is returned: K+B+rook-pawn vs bare king with the bishop not controlling the promotion corner and the enemy king within two squares of it → 0; no pawns and ≤ a minor piece ahead → score/8; opposite-coloured bishops with pawns → score/2 in the eg part. This alone would have stopped 35.Ne5/37.bxc6 in 03f 23, because the liquidation would have scored 0 against a still-positive alternative (Ke4). Add an endgame king term: −3 per square of king distance to the nearest passed pawn (own or enemy) and +2 per square of centralisation once non-pawn material ≤ R+minor per side, so Ke4 (03f 23) and Kd2-e3-d4 (b46 21) outrank shuffling.

4. **Spend the clock** (`search.rs`). `03fdcdc` used 63% of 0.25 s on average; the six slip moves in games 27 and 29 were all depth 8, three of them stopped at 0.11-0.16 s. Raise the soft limit so an iteration is started while elapsed < 0.7 × movetime, always finish the current iteration up to the hard limit, and extend by one iteration when the best move changed in the last completed one. This is the cheapest ply available and the one that would have seen 20...gxf6 (03f 4) and 19.e4 (03f 5), both eight-ply tactics missed at d8/0.24 s.

5. **Patch the king-safety term's three blind spots** (`eval.rs::king_danger`). (a) Count shield pawns only on absolute ranks 2-3 (7-6 for Black), so a king on f2 gets no credit for a pawn on g4 (03f 5, 19.hxg4). (b) Weight an attack on a zone square that is defended by a pawn at zero and on an empty or piece-occupied zone square at full, which removes the Bh6-vs-g7 phantom (03f 27, 23.Nd3). (c) Start the `DANGER` table at one attacker with a small value (e.g. 6 for weight 2-3) so the term has a gradient, and count an enemy pawn on the sixth rank in or next to the zone as an attacker (item 1). Leave `SHIELD_MISSING` where it is until (a)-(c) are measured; the `498a47b` experience says raising it alone brings needless king moves.

Not recommended now: changing the repetition score itself. In these two runs the 0 score never cost a point; it earned five draws from lost positions. The horizon case (b46 24, 03f 5 28.Qxh7+) is better handled by a small penalty in the check-extension path for a sequence of ≥3 consecutive checks by the side to move that gains material but does not mate, or simply by the extra depth of item 4.
