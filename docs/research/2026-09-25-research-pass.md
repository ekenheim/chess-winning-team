### 5s-games

**Where the points went.** The 4 losses were all decided in the opening or middlegame, and the engine never had a winning position in any of them (Stockfish never rated it +1.5 or better). In every loss the engine's own eval stayed at or above 0 while Stockfish had it at -300 to -600. The misjudgements are about king safety and development, not deep tactics:
- opening the h-file toward its own king (games 17 and 26)
- an opening queen raid with the king left in the centre (game 22)
- a slow king-side drift (game 4)

**The 7 draws.**
- 3 draws came from positions Stockfish rated +1.5 or better for us. Only 1 of them reached +3 (game 7, +409).
- 3 draws were saves from lost positions (games 5, 16 and 28, at -650 to -854).
- 1 draw was level (game 12).
- The two slipped endgames (games 21 and 24) fail the same way. The engine scored dead-drawn endings at +2.5 to +6 for 100+ plies: a wrong-coloured bishop with an a-pawn, rook-vs-rook with doubled h-pawns, and knight vs bare king. So it has no draw-scaling for known drawn endgames and traded down into them.

**Move quality by phase.** Middlegame is the weak phase: 37.0 average centipawn loss (ACPL) at average depth 13.0, against 14.0 ACPL at depth 18.1 in the endgame. Of the 38 blunders (200+ cp), 36 came at depth 15 or less.

**Implications for 5 s play.**
1. Endgame draw-scaling in the eval would target the slipped draws directly.
2. King-safety terms would target the losses. This is what Erik's king-danger-v3 patch aims at.
3. Getting deeper in the middlegame matters more than anything in the endgame: the middlegame is at depth 13 even with 5 s.

### search-logs

I mined moves.jsonl for the four 5 s runs (7047219, b46124c, 55375f9, 555bcaf) and for the 0.25 s runs. I read the CHAMP time manager (search.rs lines 145-208). I also re-ran the champion binary on 30 positions sampled from 555bcaf-full with `go movetime 5000` to log each iteration, and ran the same positions at fixed depth with a 4x TT build (scratch copy only).

Main points:
1. Going from 0.25 s to 5 s (20x the time) gains only about 3.5-4 plies in the middlegame, because the effective branching factor is about 2.0. In endgames the gain is about 10 plies.
2. 41% of all engine time in 555bcaf-full goes into iterations that never finish, and 13% of the budget sits unused because of the 60% soft stop.
3. The move played changes very little after about 1 s.
4. The same engine scored the same (0.75) on the same 2100-2300 ladder at 0.25 s (run c9cb33a) and at 5 s (555bcaf-full). The extra time has not yet turned into Elo against this opponent.
5. The draws left on the table come from missing endgame knowledge in the eval, which extra depth cannot fix.

Nothing was committed or modified outside the scratch dirs. Scripts and data are in /private/tmp/claude-501/research/scratch/: mine.py, mine2.py, waste.py, low.py, instab.py, iters.py and iters.json (per-iteration logs), fixd.py (TT test), pos_*.json, and tt/ (the 1<<23 TT build).

### code-audit

I audited the champion engine at 555bcaf, which is 959 lines in engine/src/{search.rs, eval.rs, main.rs}. Its search is small: iterative deepening, PVS, a single-slot always-replace TT (2^21 x 24 B = 48 MB), null move (R = 2 + d/4), a very mild LMR, check extension, killers, butterfly history and MVV-LVA. The quiescence search does its own full move generation and has no SEE, no delta pruning and no TT probe. None of these are present: aspiration windows, reverse futility, futility, razoring, late move pruning, IIR, singular extensions, countermove or continuation history, SEE, mate distance pruning, pawn hash or a triangular PV. The eval is Michniewski PST (piece-square tables) plus material, a tapered pawn endgame table and pawn-shield/king-zone king safety. It has no passed-pawn, doubled/isolated, mobility, bishop-pair or rook-file terms and no endgame scaling or draw recognition. The eval is colour-symmetric; I found no correctness bug in TT mate scores.

Instrumented bench 12: 88% of nodes are in quiescence. 10.5M of the 16.1M non-first moves are quiet, non-checking moves at depth 1, which no pruning touches. LMR reduces only 7.6% of late moves. I prototyped one speed fix in scratch: generating only captures in quiescence gives +8.5% nps with identical node counts. The biggest Elo levers are pruning (reverse futility, futility, LMP, log-based LMR) and endgame draw knowledge. Two real draws in 555bcaf-full came from dead-drawn endgames the engine scored as +400. Games 16 and 21 are in /private/tmp/claude-501/research/data/games/runs/555bcaf-full/. The instrumented copies are in /private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-regent-chess-winning-team/66a65186-d64a-4d46-9313-f9800f4cd360/scratchpad/{prof,fast}. Nothing was committed or pushed, and CHAMP was not rebuilt.

- **Quiescence makes up 88% of all nodes, and 68% of those stop at the first stand-pat check. Most of the time goes into playing moves only to evaluate them and stop.** — Instrumented copy (scratchpad/prof), bench depth 12, 43,017,925 nodes: negamax 5,168,012, qsearch 37,849,913, stand-pat cutoffs 25,915,235, qsearch move generations 11,934,678, qsearch moves played 22,536,774. → Pruning near the leaves (futility at depth 1-2, reverse futility/static null move at depth <= 6-8, delta pruning and SEE<0 capture pruning in quiescence) removes a large share of the nodes. At 5 s/move that should give several plies. Median depth is 14 now (555bcaf-full moves.jsonl, 1709 engine moves).
- **Almost no frontier pruning, and LMR is extremely conservative: only 7.6% of late moves are reduced, and never by more than 2 plies.** — search.rs:27-30,343-351: r=1 when depth>=3 and move index>=3, r=2 only at index>=8 and depth>=6. Counters: 16,100,689 non-first moves, 1,217,779 reduced, 102,652 reduced by 2, LMR re-searches only 1,416. 12,595,496 non-first moves are at depth 1, where LMR never applies, and 10,514,636 of those are quiet and non-checking. First-move cutoff rate is 2,925,309/3,144,568 = 93%. → With 93% first-move cutoffs, ordering is good enough for a published log-based LMR (r ~ 0.75 + ln(d)*ln(i)/2.25, less in PV nodes, less for good history), plus late move pruning of quiet moves at low depth. Only 1,416 re-searches shows the current reductions are nearly never wrong, so they are far too timid. This is likely the biggest single depth gain.
- **The eval has no endgame or draw knowledge. It scored book draws at +400 for 30+ moves, and two of the seven draws in the champion run trace to this.** — 555bcaf-full game_21 (fifty-move draw): the engine held +404/+405 at depth 21-33 for its last 10 moves. The final position 8/Pk6/8/3K4/8/8/5B2/8 (wrong-colour bishop plus rook pawn) evaluates to +582. Game_16 (insufficient material): the engine reported +390 and ended in KN v K, 8/6K1/8/5k2/8/8/8/3n4, which evaluates to +338 for the knight side. The eval command on these FENs gives 582 and -338. → Add insufficient-material detection (KNK, KBK, KNNK) returning 0, and scale the eval toward 0 when the side ahead has no pawns and less than a rook of extra material. Add wrong-bishop rook-pawn and opposite-coloured-bishop scaling. Then the engine will keep its pawns instead of trading into dead draws. It also stops search time being wasted on false '+4' lines.
- **The eval has almost no positional terms. There are no passed-pawn terms beyond a flat rank table, and no pawn structure, mobility, bishop pair or rook-file terms.** — eval.rs:124-133: PAWN_EG_PST rewards every pawn by rank (90/55/30/15/5), passed or not. eval.rs:226-250 evaluate() is material + Michniewski PST + king_danger only. There is no pawn hash anywhere. → Obvious hand-written terms to add: passed pawns (by rank, free path, king distance in the endgame), isolated/doubled pawns, bishop pair (~30-50 cp), rooks on open/semi-open files, and a cheap mobility count. Pawn-structure terms should go behind a small pawn hash table. Per the originality rule, the weights should be self-chosen or tuned with our own games, not taken from any engine.
- **Profile of bench 13: move generation and sorting take about 52% of the time, evaluate about 25%, play_unchecked about 11%, malloc/free about 5%.** — macOS `sample` on bench 13 (109M nodes, 5.60M nps), about 5.7k samples: ordered_moves closure 1606 + ordered_moves 717 + quicksort 420 + insertion sort 94 + add_king_legals 92 + can_castle 59; evaluate 1405; play_unchecked 643; malloc/free about 270; negamax 264; quiescence 224. Microbenchmark: evaluate 47 ns/call (27.5 ns without king_danger), full legal movegen 31 ns/call. → The quiescence search (search.rs:430 through ordered_moves, search.rs:480-493) generates every legal move, including quiets and castling, then throws the quiets away. Evaluate spends about 40% of its time in king_danger, which runs slider attack lookups for every enemy piece on every call.
- **Measured speed win: masking the cozy-chess PieceMoves.to bitboard to captures only in quiescence gives +8.5% nps with a bit-identical search.** — Scratch copy scratchpad/fast/src/search.rs: in captures_only mode, `moves.to &= enemy | (pawn ? back_rank | ep_square : 0)` before iterating. Bench 12 gives the same 43,017,925 nodes, 6.73 s vs 7.31 s (6.39M vs 5.88M nps), repeated twice. Replacing the Vec with a 256-entry stack array was slower (5.83-6.0M nps), because initialising or copying the 2 KB array cost more than the allocation. → This is a free, zero-risk change to adopt. Other speed ideas, not implemented: try the hash move before generating any moves (93% first-move cutoffs, many of them from the hash move); pick moves lazily instead of fully sorting; merge value+PST into one precomputed [color][piece][sq] (mg,eg) table; lazy eval that skips king_danger when material+PST is far outside the window. Erik's incremental eval measured +1.2 ply before (ledger 3f28668) and is worth retrying now that the search is stronger.
- **The TT is a single slot per index with always-replace, no depth preference and no aging, at 2M entries. Quiescence never probes or stores it, and TT exact cutoffs almost never happen.** — search.rs:41-60,281-291,401-407. Counters for bench 12 (fresh TT per position): 1,529,746 hits, 724,209 slots found occupied by a different key, 325 exact cutoffs. In 5 s games about 17M nodes/move (555bcaf-full game 1 ply 1: 17,551,941 nodes) means about 2M+ interior stores/move, the size of the whole table. Peak RAM is 52.7 MB (summary.txt). → Use 2-4 entry buckets with depth+age replacement. Pack entries to 16 B and enlarge the table if memory rules allow (e.g. 128-256 MB). Also probe the TT in quiescence. Deep entries from earlier iterations and moves then survive, which matters more at 5 s than at 0.25 s.
- **Root search always uses a full window, and there is no PV table.    (b) Futility: `depth <= 3 && static_eval + FUT_MARGIN[depth as usize] <= alpha`

   After a quiet move is searched (not pruned), `quiets_seen += 1`. Reuse gives_check in the existing LMR condition in place of child.checkers().is_empty().

### P2: movetime time use + root aspiration windows

```

let mut delta = 25; let (mut a, mut b) = if use_asp {(best_score-delta, best_score+delta)} else {(-INF, INF)};

  break
}
```

## All candidate ideas

- **Frontier pruning package: reverse futility (static null move), futility pruning and late-move pruning** (search, M, +60 to +120 (the largest single search lever; published results for this family are typically +1.5-3 plies at equal time, and middlegame depth 13 should reach ~15-16)): Add the standard published selective-pruning family, which is completely absent from the champion. (a) Reverse futility pruning: at non-PV, not-in-check nodes with depth <= 6 (8 once it is proven), return static_eval when static_eval - margin*depth >= beta, provided beta is not a mate score. (b) Futility pruning: at depth <= 3, once one move has been searched, skip quiet non-checking moves (not killers, not promotions) when static_eval + fmargin[depth] <= alpha. (c) Late-move pruning: at depth <= 4-5 in non-PV nodes, stop searching quiet moves after the first 3 + depth*depth of them. Compute static_eval once per node and reuse it in the NMP gate, which currently calls evaluate() again. Add an 'improving' flag, which needs static_eval stored per ply: when improving, loosen LMP and tighten RFP. Choose our own margins (starting points ~90 cp/ply for RFP; 100/200/300 for futility) and tune them on our own node-to-depth and game data only. Evidence: code-audit: 88% of nodes are in qsearch, and 68% of those stand pat at once. 10.5M of 16.1M non-first moves are quiet, non-checking moves at depth 1 that nothing prunes. A grep for futility/razor/reverse finds nothing, and no reviewer ever proposed these (history sweep). search-logs: the effective branching factor is about 1.96, and 20x the time bought only +3.5 plies in the middlegame. The middlegame is the weak phase (ACPL 37-43 at depth 13), and 36 of 38 blunders came at depth <= 15. Prior: none (never tried on either branch; the ledger only has NMP + LMR in 8e592f4/b46124c)
- **Log-based LMR table with PV/history/improving adjustments** (search, S, +40 to +80 (roughly +1-2 plies in the middlegame at 5 s)): Replace the 2-step LMR (r=1 from move index 3 at depth>=3, r=2 from index 8 at depth>=6) with a precomputed table r[d][i] = floor(0.75 + ln(d)*ln(i)/2.25), with our own constants to be tuned. Reduce by one ply less at PV nodes, when in check or giving check, for killers, and when !improving is false. Adjust by history as well: r -= hist/some_scale, clamped so that a reduced depth is at least 1. Also allow a small reduction (r-1) for late captures that SEE marks as losing, once SEE exists. Keep the existing re-search chain: reduced null window, then full depth null window, then full window at PV. Evidence: code-audit: only 7.6% of late moves are reduced, only 102k of them by 2, and there are only 1,416 LMR re-searches out of 1.2M reductions. The reductions are almost never wrong, so they are far too timid. First-move cutoff rate is 93%, so ordering is good enough to support deeper reductions. The LMR constants in 8e592f4 were described as 'first guesses, to be measured'. search-logs: EBF ~2.0; each 0.1 off the EBF is worth more plies at 5 s than at 0.25 s. Prior: 8e592f4 (sep25-robin, keep: introduced the current timid LMR); b46124c merge. The formula-based table has never been tried.
- **Qsearch and ordering: own SEE, bad-capture pruning, delta pruning, captures-only generation** (search, M, +30 to +60 (qsearch node reduction of ~30-50% plus 8.5% raw nps; better capture ordering also feeds LMR and futility)): Write our own static exchange evaluator, using the standard swap-list algorithm over cozy-chess attack bitboards with x-ray re-scan after each capture. Use it in three places. (1) Quiescence: skip captures with SEE < 0 when not in check, and apply delta pruning: skip a capture when stand_pat + victim_value + ~200 cp < alpha, except for promotions. (2) Main-search ordering: split captures into good (SEE >= 0, ordered by MVV-LVA above the killers) and bad (SEE < 0, ordered after the quiet moves). (3) At depth <= 3-4 in non-PV nodes, prune captures with SEE < -margin*depth. Also adopt the proven zero-risk speed patch: in qsearch mode, mask PieceMoves.to to enemy squares plus the promotion rank plus the en-passant square before iterating. Fix en passant being treated as quiet in the LMR, killer and history checks as well. Evidence: code-audit: 88% of nodes are quiescence nodes, and the qsearch generates every legal move and then filters (ordered_moves at search.rs:430/480-493). The captures-only mask gave +8.5% nps with identical node counts (43,017,925 nodes, 6.39M vs 5.88M nps, scratchpad/fast). The 63ccb08, 498eace, ad95e70 and dd6c58b engine-dev reviews all asked for SEE, and it was never built. En passant counts as quiet at search.rs:332 (quiet = !enemy.has(mv.to)). Prior: none for SEE and delta pruning. The capture mask was only prototyped in scratch (code-audit), never benched in a run.
- **Aspiration windows at the root, with a safe root-move rule and use of the idle soft-limit time** (time, S, +20 to +50 (aspiration windows are worth ~5-15% of nodes per iteration; the soft-limit change reclaims ~13% of the budget; together that is ~0.5-1 ply more completed per move)): From depth >= 5, search the root with alpha/beta = prev_score ∓ delta (delta ~20-30 cp, self-chosen). On a fail-low or fail-high, widen that side by delta *= 1.5-2 and re-search, until a window of full width. Two points are needed with the champion's aborted-iteration rule.
